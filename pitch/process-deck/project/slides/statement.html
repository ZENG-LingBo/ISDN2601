Problem statement: 4 versions

1. First draft

University friend groups want to spend time together, but their plans die in the group chat. No one owns the plan, it never gets a time or place, and cancelling feels like relief. So free time gets spent scrolling alone, and close friends drift apart.

Changed because: too long to say aloud easily. “Die” and “drift apart” are metaphors. The causes belong on earlier slides.

2. After a wording review

The problem: university friend groups want to spend time together, but their plans fall through in the group chat. Instead, they spend their free time alone on their phones.

Changed because: a pitch coach found it never said “problem statement”, and “free time”, the link to the theme, came last.

3. After the pitch coach

Problem statement: university friend groups want to spend their free time together, but their plans fall through in the group chat. Instead, they spend it alone on their phones.

Changed because: the new insight. Even when a plan moves forward, friends with food or money limits stay quiet.

4. Now

Problem statement: university friend groups want to spend their free time together, but their plans fall through in the group chat, and friends with food or money limits stay quiet. Instead, they spend it alone on their phones.

It still names no solution. It names the user, the problem and the result.
