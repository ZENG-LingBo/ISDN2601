Research: why plans in a group fall through

No one is in charge

The more people who could act, the less each one feels it’s their job (Darley & Latané, 1968).

In the chat: five people like the idea, and each one waits for someone else to pick a date.

No set time or place

Plans that say when and where are much more likely to happen. 94 studies, 8,000 people (Gollwitzer & Sheeran, 2006).

In the chat: “soon” and “after midterms” are wishes, not plans.

Fear of looking too eager

People underestimate how much others like them and want to hear from them (Boothby et al., 2018; Liu et al., 2023).

In the chat: “If I keep asking, I’ll seem desperate.”

Friendships need time

Two people need about 200 hours together to become close friends (Hall, 2019).

In the chat: every plan that falls through is time the friendship doesn’t get.

Numbers alone don't explain why plans fall through, so I looked for psychology research on the causes. I found three causes and one reason it matters. For each one, the grey line shows what it looks like in a real group chat. Darley and Latané studied emergencies, not group chats, so I'm applying their idea to a new setting.
