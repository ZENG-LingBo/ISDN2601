Target user: from the evidence to university friend groups

Young people in Hong Kong

Why: 83.7% report moderate to high loneliness (HKFYG, 2026).

Left out: young people in their first jobs. They were the loneliest group, but I can’t easily reach them to test ideas.

University students

Why: students score higher on loneliness (Hong Kong Family Welfare Society, 2026). Almost half of first-years think others have made more close friends (Whillans et al., 2017).

Left out: secondary school students, whose free time is mostly set by school and family.

Year 2 and up

Why: Year 1 has orientation camp, halls and shared classes. By Year 2 these fade, but the friendships are still new (Hall, 2019).

Left out: Year 1 students, who still meet without planning.

Friend groups of 4 to 6

Why: big enough that everyone waits for someone else (Darley & Latané, 1968), small enough to share one table.

Left out: clubs and societies, which have leaders whose job is to plan.

I narrowed the evidence in four steps. At each step I wrote down why I kept a group, and who I left out. The loneliest group in the HKFYG survey was young people in their first jobs, but I chose students because I can reach them and test ideas with them. Year 2 matters because the ready-made chances to meet from Year 1 are gone, while the friendships are still new.
