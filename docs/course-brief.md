# Course brief and checklist

Read this before changing the pitch, the game or the website, so the project stays on brief.

## The brief

From the original request. The end of the pasted brief was cut off, so anything after the first research task is missing. If the course has a separate score sheet, add it here.

- **Course:** ISDN 1001 "Introduction to Integrative Systems and Design", HKUST (Prof. CY Tsui, Mr. Terence Cheung). Individual project.
- **Theme:** "Leisure, Entertainment and Culture in 2030": moving from killing time to intentional, meaningful downtime.
  - **Trends in the brief:** live and in-person experiences, personalisation, immersive tech, edutainment.
  - **Enabling tech:** embedded AI, IoT, wearables, smart sensing, cloud and edge.
- **The pitch:** one confirmed problem statement plus the background study that supports it. 2 minutes or less. No solution or product.
- **Graded on design thinking:**
  - target user
  - pain points
  - empathy map (says, does, thinks, feels)
  - How Might We questions
  - storytelling
- **How Might We rules from the lecturers:**
  - a specific user and a specific problem
  - no solution inside it
  - broad enough for many ideas
  - focused on the outcome you want
  - worded positively
  - doesn't lead only to "providing information"
  - Template: "How might we [specific task] for [target audience] in order to [gain or pain relief]?"
- **Storytelling rules:**
  - open with the user's problem, not the product
  - simple words
  - emotion
  - end with a clear statement
- **Classmates' topics to avoid:**
  - remote workers
  - gamers
  - gym goers
  - pet owners
  - urban families
  - walkers
  - young elderly
  - short waiting times

## The professor's new direction (after the first topic was turned down)

Invent a new activity, sport or game that reduces these problems with today's activities:
- the work of gathering people
- having to learn the game
- having to buy expensive equipment
- the risk of getting hurt
