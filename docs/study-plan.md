# One-week study plan

A simple plan for testing Minibus with real friend groups in the next stage of ISDN 1001. With only 4 to 6 groups, this is a qualitative study: it shows how groups use the app and where they get stuck. It can't prove that Minibus works better than other tools.

## The question

When a friend group says "we should hang out", does Minibus help them actually meet, compared with what they normally do?

## Who takes part

- 4 to 6 friend groups of 4 to 6 people each, aged 18 or over.
- Each group talked about a plan in their group chat in the last month but never set a time.
- At least 2 groups that aren't your own friends.
- A mix of iPhone and Android.

Get your instructor's written approval and a signed consent form from every person first. The consent checklist is in [`prototype/README.md`](../prototype/README.md#consent-for-a-user-test).

## The comparison

Split the groups into two halves:

- **Half use Minibus** for their next plan.
- **Half use what they normally do**, plus a WhatsApp poll for the time.

If you only have 4 groups, run Minibus with all of them and use the baseline survey (below) as the comparison instead. Say so clearly in the report.

## Before the week: baseline survey

Ask every participant 3 questions:

1. In the last 4 weeks, how many times did someone in this group say "we should hang out" (or similar)?
2. How many of those times did you actually meet?
3. When a plan in this group gets cancelled, how do you usually feel? (relieved, disappointed, both, neither)

## During the week

Each group starts one real plan in its own chat. Don't remind the Minibus groups to use the app. What to measure:

| Step | Minibus (from the export) | WhatsApp group (ask the organiser) |
|---|---|---|
| Opened the link | link opens | — |
| Answered | answers, time of each first answer | poll votes |
| Picked a time | first confirmation and how it happened | did the group agree on a time? |
| Changed plans | how often the plan moved or went back to choosing a time, each person's "can't make this time" answer, and the notice the plan showed last | did anyone cancel? |
| Met | check-ins, "did you meet?" answers | did they meet? (confirm with 2 people) |

Download the export every day ([how](../prototype/README.md#back-up-and-download-the-data)).

## After the week: short interviews

10 minutes with 1 or 2 people from each group:

- What happened with the plan, in your own words?
- Was there a moment you felt awkward or pushy? When?
- Did seeing only numbers, not names, change what you answered?
- Would you rather see no numbers until the plan is confirmed?
- Would you use this again for the next plan? Why or why not?

## What to report

- For each group: the steps it reached, and how long each took.
- Quotes from the interviews, grouped by the 3 causes from the research: no one in charge, no set time or place, and fear of looking too eager.
- What didn't work, and what you would change.
- Limits: a small number of groups, a short time, and friends of the researcher in the sample.

## Ethics

- Only collect what's listed in the consent form.
- Don't collect screenshots of group chats; they include people who didn't agree to take part.
- Use group numbers, not names, in the report.
- Delete the data by the date you promised.
