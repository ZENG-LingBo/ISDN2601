# Why young people stop playing sport after school: sources

Background research for the new direction: a new low-barrier group game. Gathered on 26 Nov 2026.

**Read this first:** none of these pages could be opened from the research session, because the hosts are outside its network. Every entry comes from search results and summaries only. Open each source yourself and check the exact numbers before you put it on a slide.

## 1. Activity drops after school

- **Guthold R, Stevens GA, Riley LM, Bull FC (2020).** Global trends in insufficient physical activity among adolescents: a pooled analysis of 298 population-based surveys with 1.6 million participants. *Lancet Child & Adolescent Health*, 4(1), 23–35. In 2016, 81.0% of school-going 11–17-year-olds were not active enough (boys 77.6%, girls 84.7%), in 146 countries. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31761562/
- **Corder K et al. (2019).** Change in physical activity from adolescence to early adulthood: a systematic review and meta-analysis of longitudinal cohort studies. *British Journal of Sports Medicine*, 53(8), 496–503. 67 studies of ages 13–30. Activity "declines modestly". https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6250429/
- **Winpenny EM et al. (2020).** Changes in physical activity, diet and body weight across the education and employment transitions of early adulthood. *Obesity Reviews*. Leaving high school was linked to about 7 fewer minutes a day of moderate-to-vigorous activity (−7.04 min/day, 95% CI −11.26 to −2.82; 9 studies). Leaving university showed no change. https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7079102/
- **LCSD / Community Sports Committee (2023).** Territory-wide Physical Fitness Survey for the Community, fieldwork 2021–22. 9,326 people aged 7–79. 53.8% of adults didn't meet the WHO target of 150 minutes a week. For ages 17–59, the main barriers were lack of time, tiredness and laziness. Government press release, 16 Jun 2023. https://www.info.gov.hk/gia/general/202306/16/P2023061600260p.htm and https://www.legco.gov.hk/yr2023/english/panels/ha/papers/ha20230612cb2-539-5-e.pdf
- **Abdullah ASM, Wong CM, Yam HK, Fielding R (2005).** Factors related to non-participation in physical activity among the students in Hong Kong. *International Journal of Sports Medicine*, 26(7), 611–615. A 1997 survey of Year 1 and Year 3 undergraduates found about one-third inactive. The top reasons were "no time" and "no interest". The data is old. https://www.thieme-connect.de/products/ejournals/abstract/10.1055/s-2004-821315

## 2. Being bad in front of others

- **Hart EA, Leary MR, Rejeski WJ (1989).** The measurement of social physique anxiety. *Journal of Sport & Exercise Psychology*, 11(1), 94–104. It builds a scale and shows anxiety during a real physique evaluation. It measures the anxiety but doesn't show that people drop out because of it. https://scholars.duke.edu/publication/676992
- **Crane J, Temple V (2015).** A systematic review of dropout from organized sport among children and youth. *European Physical Education Review*, 21(1), 114–131. Personal and social reasons, such as feeling less skilled than others and not enjoying it, outweighed practical ones. Check the authors' names against the paper. https://lida.sport-iat.de/twm/Record/4086628
- **Back J, Johnson U, Svedberg P, McCall A, Ivarsson A (2022).** Drop-out from team sport among adolescents: a systematic review and meta-analysis. *Psychology of Sport and Exercise*, 61, 102205. In 12 studies (6,304 players), motivation and experience in the sport were most strongly linked to dropout. https://opus.lib.uts.edu.au/handle/10453/162294
- **Cardinal BJ, Yan Z, Cardinal MK (2013).** Negative experiences in physical education and sport. *Journal of Physical Education, Recreation & Dance*, 84(3), 49–53. In 293 students, those who were picked last for teams reported less physical activity later in life. This is a recall survey, so it shows a link, not a cause. https://scholarworks.merrimack.edu/health_facpubs/23

## 3. Time, coordination and booking

- **Silva RMF et al. (2022).** Barriers to high school and university students' physical activity: a systematic review. *PLOS ONE*, 17(4), e0265913. In 59 studies, 22 of them with university students, the top barriers were lack of time, lack of motivation and lack of places. https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8979430/
- **HKFYG Youth Research Centre (2016).** YI016, an NGO poll of 520 secondary students. Of the 263 who did no extracurricular sport, 49.4% said they had no time. https://yrc.hkfyg.org.hk/wp-content/uploads/sites/56/2021/10/YI016_Summary_Eng.pdf
- **Booking Hong Kong courts (news and government releases).**
  - The LCSD confirmed that bot accounts were booking indoor venues on SmartPLAY (SCMP). https://www.scmp.com/news/hong-kong/society/article/3341941/calls-stricter-rules-after-bot-bookers-detected-hong-kong-sports-app
  - Bot users now face a 360-day ban, and the LCSD stopped letting people pick up unused basketball and volleyball slots because of scalping. https://www.thestandard.com.hk/news/article/322139/ and https://www.info.gov.hk/gia/general/202512/22/P2025122200238p.htm
  - No figures were found on demand compared with the number of slots.
- **Not found:** the share of students who say they have no one to play with.

## 4. Cost

- **Edwards G, Grubb B, Power A, Serle N (2015).** *Moving the goal posts: poverty and access to sport for young people.* LSE CASEreport 95 / StreetGames. Interviews with about 135 people aged 14–25 in poorer UK areas. Fees and kit were the biggest barrier, and leaving school was a "cliff edge". https://eprints.lse.ac.uk/67852
- **Hong Kong:** no good evidence was found. Public fees are low (for example, about HK$51–59 an hour for pickleball), and cost doesn't appear among the barriers in the LCSD survey. **Cost is a weak point for Hong Kong.**

## 5. Injury

- **Fong DT et al. (2007).** A systematic review on ankle injury and ankle sprain in sports. *Sports Medicine*, 37(1), 73–94. 227 studies in 70 sports. The ankle is the second most injured part of the body, and sprains are most common in court and team sports. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17190537/
- **Fong DT, Man CY, Yung PS, Cheung SY, Chan KM (2008).** Sport-related ankle injuries attending an accident and emergency department. *Injury*, 39(10), 1222–1227. 1,715 sports injuries at Prince of Wales Hospital A&E in 2005. Of 240 ankle injuries, 32.9% came from basketball and 31.7% from football. https://repository.lboro.ac.uk/articles/Sport-related_ankle_injuries_attending_an_accident_and_emergency_department/9628274
- **Fear of injury as a reason not to play:** the evidence is weak. In student surveys, about 10–13% mention it, far below lack of time.

## 6. Screens

- **Department of Health, Student Health Service, 2023/24 report** (press release, 2 Apr 2025). About 80% of secondary and 43% of primary students spent 2 or more hours a day on screens outside school. https://www.info.gov.hk/gia/general/202504/02/P2025040200311.htm
- **Huang WY et al. (2023).** Hong Kong 2022 Report Card. *Journal of Exercise Science & Fitness*. Overall physical activity got a D−: 25% of children and adolescents met the guidelines, measured by device. Screen time got a D: 28% kept it to 2 hours a day or less. https://www.cpr.cuhk.edu.hk/wp-content/uploads/newscentre/pressrelease/Grades-of-the-2022-Hong-Kong-Report-Card.pdf
- Don't use the "6–7 hours a day" teen figure. It came from a political party seminar, and no primary source was found.

## 7. People want gentler, easier sports

- **SFIA (2026).** Topline Participation Report (an industry body). 24.3 million US pickleball players in 2025, up 22.8% on 2024. https://sfia.org/research/u-s-pickleball-participation/
- **LCSD (2025).** Opened free outdoor badminton courts and six sports-centre arenas to pickleball from 15 Jan 2025, citing rising interest. https://www.lcsd.gov.hk/en/facilities/facilitieslist/landsports/freeoutdoor/opening_up_badminton_courts.html
- **Walking football.** Price et al. (2025), *Sports*: injury rates were "low" over more than 6,000 hours at 7 English clubs. https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12115786/
- **Caution:** US emergency visits for pickleball injuries rose 91% from 2020 to 2022, mostly among people over 50. Describe pickleball as easy to learn, not as low-injury.

## What this means for the story

- **Strongest:** time and coordination, motivation, and feeling less skilled than others (being picked last, not enjoying it). These are the top reasons people give, in Hong Kong and worldwide.
- **Weaker than expected:** cost (public fees in Hong Kong are low) and fear of injury (about 1 in 10 students). Keep them in the design, but don't lead the story with them.
- **Still to find:** Hong Kong university students' own reasons, and how many have no one to play with. Interviews can cover both.

## Five facts for a slide (check each source first)

1. Worldwide, 81% of school-going 11–17-year-olds aren't active enough. (Guthold et al., 2020)
2. 53.8% of Hong Kong adults miss the WHO activity target, and lack of time is the top reason. (LCSD, 2023)
3. Leaving high school is linked to about 7 fewer active minutes a day. (Winpenny et al., 2020)
4. Students picked last for teams reported less physical activity later in life. (Cardinal et al., 2013)
5. Basketball and football caused 65% of sport ankle injuries seen at one Hong Kong A&E. (Fong et al., 2008)
